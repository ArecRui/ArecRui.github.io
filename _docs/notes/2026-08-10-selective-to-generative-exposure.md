Selective exposure — the tendency to seek information that confirms what we already believe — has anchored media-effects research since the era of print and broadcasting. Li Xigen's three-element model gave that tendency a tidy structure: the person, the medium, and the message, interacting under a specific set of motivations.

My automated scanning pipeline complicates the model in a way I did not design for. Every morning it surfaces papers I would never have clicked: adjacent fields, awkward methods, journals outside my routine. The exposure is no longer something I *select*; it is something the system *generates* and hands to me. Call it generative exposure.

This note is a working attempt to slot generative exposure into the three-element frame without breaking it — treating the recommender or agent as a new kind of "medium" whose selectivity operates on my behalf, often below awareness. More to come as the data accumulates.
